# Few-shot spoken-intent matching on Wolof without transcription: WolBanking77 measurement

Generated by `uv run fewshot all` (see `research/harness/README.md`). Tables and inline figures are read
from `wolbanking77-fewshot.results.json` when this file is built. Protocol: `protocol.md`, written before
any result was seen; deviations are listed there and in section 9.

## 1. Bottom line

With k recordings per intent and a frozen Whisper-tiny or Whisper-base encoder, mean-pooled and matched
by cosine similarity, **the matcher does not work well enough to use, on this dataset**:

- Best pre-registered configuration (base / final / nn), new speakers, k=10: macro accuracy
  63.9% (chance 10%), per-clip accuracy
  53.2%. At k=1 it is
  29.4% macro. When the query's sentence was not among the
  support recordings (condition B), k=10 macro accuracy is 46.6%.
- Whisper-tiny and Whisper-base are indistinguishable here (k=10 macro
  63.3% vs 63.9%,
  with an episode standard deviation of about 3 points).
- **Abstention by cosine threshold does not rescue it.** A threshold calibrated on held-out speakers for
  "at most 5% error among accepted" accepts
  3.5% of utterances at k=10,
  and the accepted ones are still wrong
  10.3% of the
  time. Even with the threshold chosen on the test data itself (an upper bound), coverage at 5% error is
  2.4%.
- The vectors are not noise. A post-hoc check (section 6) with every recording of 6 other speakers as
  support (about 1245 clips, far
  from few-shot) reaches 85.3% macro
  accuracy on new speakers. The matcher behaves more like "have I heard this sentence before?" than
  "what does this mean?": it needs the phrasing to be in the support set, and k examples per intent do
  not cover the 2 to 39 phrasings each intent has in this data.

What this does and does not say about the maternal-health tool is in section 8.

## 3. Protocol in brief

Full text in `protocol.md`. In each of 50 seeded episodes the 16 speakers are split 6 / 4 / 6 into
support pool / calibration / test, with no speaker in two groups (3 draws were rejected for
not covering all intents). k in {1, 3, 5, 10} support clips per intent are drawn from the support pool.
Encoder vectors are the mean over the encoder frames that cover real audio (not the 30 s padding),
L2-normalised. Two classifiers: prototype-mean, and nearest-neighbour over all support clips.

- **Condition A**: every clip of the 6 test speakers (about 1159 per
  episode). The same sentence, read by someone else, may be in the support set.
- **Condition B**: only test clips whose sentence is not in the support set. At larger k the intents with
  few sentences drop out, so B is over fewer intents (column "B intents left").

The configuration used for robustness and export was chosen by macro accuracy on the calibration
speakers only. Thresholds for abstention were chosen on calibration speakers and applied to test
speakers; "oracle" numbers choose the threshold on the test data and are upper bounds.

## 8. Limits

- **The dataset is too small and too narrow for any claim about a health tool.** 16 speaker ids, 177
  read sentences, 10 banking/transport intents, about 4.3 hours. Nothing here is health vocabulary,
  nothing is spontaneous speech, none of the speakers is known to be pregnant, and recording conditions
  are unknown.
- **What can be claimed:** on this data, k <= 10 recordings per intent with mean-pooled frozen
  Whisper-tiny/base vectors gives accuracy far below what a health tool needs, and a cosine threshold
  does not buy low error at any useful coverage. That is a negative result about this particular recipe
  (mean pooling, no training, broad intents with many phrasings), measured on one small dataset.
- **What cannot be claimed:** that the idea fails in general; that it would fail when each "meaning" is
  one short fixed phrase; that it works or fails in Hindi or Marathi (not measured); how it behaves on
  real phone audio, real background noise, or far-field speech.
- **Intent granularity mismatch.** A WolBanking77 intent is a broad category covering 2 to 39 different
  sentences. The tool's "one of N known meanings" may be closer to one phrase per meaning. Section 6
  suggests the matcher behaves like a phrase matcher. That case (177 sentence classes, k recordings of
  the same sentence by other speakers) was not in the pre-registered protocol and was not measured as a
  few-shot task. It is the obvious next experiment and this dataset supports it.
- **Abstention was only tested on in-scope audio.** There are no out-of-scope utterances, so "abstain
  when the meaning is unknown", which matters most for safety, is not measured at all.
- **Speakers rotate between roles.** Across episodes the same speaker is support in one, calibration in
  another and test in a third. Episodes are therefore correlated; the ± values describe spread over
  splits of these 16 speakers and are not confidence intervals for a new population. Model selection
  used calibration speakers only within an episode, but those speakers are test speakers in other
  episodes, so the selected cell's test numbers are very slightly optimistic. All 20 cells are reported.
- **Speaker identity is taken from `user_id`**; if two ids are one person, the hold-out is weaker than
  stated.
- **Robustness is synthetic**: band-limiting and white noise, not a phone channel, a codec, or real
  background noise.
- **Latency is for one laptop CPU** under a shared desktop load, not a phone. Arm phone numbers will
  differ and were not measured. The 30 s padding is the dominant cost and was not optimised.
- **Not tried** (so no claim either way): attention or learned pooling, a small trained projection head,
  frame-level matching (DTW), other small encoders, Whisper encoders fed less than 30 s, any fine-tuning.

## 9. Deviations from the protocol

1. **Layer grid.** The protocol listed `L1..Ln` (output of each block before the final LayerNorm) plus
   `final`. `transformers` does not expose the last block's output before the final LayerNorm: its last
   hidden state already is `final`. So `Ln` and `final` were the same tensor, and the grid is `L1..L(n-1)`
   plus `final`: 4 layers for tiny, 6 for base, 20 cells instead of 24. The first grid run contained the
   duplicate under both names with identical numbers; the duplicate label was removed and the grid stage
   re-run from the cached vectors. No result changed and the selected configuration is the same tensor.
2. **Post-hoc diagnostics** (section 6) were added after the main results were seen. They are labelled
   as such and were not used for selection.
3. **Latency** was measured while other desktop processes were running, not on an idle machine.
4. Because the selected layer is `final`, only the full encoder was exported (the protocol's truncated
   export applies only to an intermediate layer).
